among the magazines and the databases

where this portal sits in the landscape of materials publishing, what the neighbours do better, and the fixes that fall out of the comparison.

the short version

Interesting Materials is neither a magazine nor a database, and that is its whole position. A magazine like IEEE Spectrum publishes a few deep, authored stories a week; a database like the Materials Project holds a hundred thousand computed compounds nobody reads for pleasure. This portal is the thing in between: a curated field guide of 539 materials, each kept for one nameable reason, browsable like a catalogue and written like an explainer, in a single design system, free. Against the magazines it wins on browsability, honesty of sourcing and cost, and loses on depth, authority signals and interactive polish. The upgrades at the end come straight from that second list.

where it wins, where it lags

It wins by doing the one thing none of the others does at once: choosing the materials, saying why each is interesting, and showing the source, inside a design that treats the reader's attention as the scarce resource. A magazine cannot be browsed; a database cannot be read; the Institute of Making cannot be opened in a tab. This portal is all three at a small size.

It lags wherever a reader wants to go deeper or compare: no long read when a card earns one, no way to plot stiffness against density, no filter by a number, no visible author to trust. These are not flaws in the idea; they are the next rooms to build, and every one has a neighbour who has shown how.
