how this is made

what earns a material its card, where every fact comes from, and the rules the page keeps. the page is anonymous on purpose; what is accountable here is the method, and the method is open.

the idea

The Bauhaus sent every student to the workshop before the drawing board: a term of handling metal, wood, paper and cloth, to learn what each will do and what it refuses to do. This catalogue continues that preliminary course. A material is here for one reason — a thing it does, a way it is made, or a way it fails — and the card names it. Ordinary is not a reason, and famous is not a reason.

four kinds of entry

  • A card is one material: a reason it is interesting, three properties, and where you actually meet it.
  • A plate is a figure that explains several cards at once — a phase diagram or a chart — drawn from data and answering the pointer.
  • A paper is the paper a claim rests on: the one that introduced a material, or a recent result that promises one, with its DOI.
  • A text is a primary source from the Bauhaus itself, in the library, linking out to a public-domain copy where one exists.

the rules it keeps

Seven principles, each drawn from a text in the library and each meant to be checked, not admired. The long form, with the enforcement, is in PRINCIPLES.md.

1. The material first, its picture second

Gropius, Bauhaus Manifesto, 1919

A card is first the behaviour of a material — what it does, how it is made, how it fails — and only then a photograph of it. The picture is evidence, not the point.

2. One reason, or it does not earn its place

Kandinsky, Point and Line to Plane, 1926

Nothing is on the page that is not doing a job. A material that is only ordinary, or only famous, is not a candidate; neither is a second card that makes the first one's point again.

3. Show the thing itself; where you cannot, say what you are showing

Josef Albers, Interaction of Color, 1963

When no free photograph of the material itself exists, the card shows the next best thing — something made from it, how it is made, a close relative — and stamps in red on the picture what it actually is. The reader is never allowed to mistake an analogue for the thing.

4. Truth to the source

Moholy-Nagy, Von Material zu Architektur, 1929

Every claim rests on something a reader can reach. Every photograph keeps its licence and its origin; every founding claim is tied to a paper; the library links out to the primary text.

5. Form is the record of a process

Paul Klee, Pedagogical Sketchbook, 1925

A material is best explained by the process that shapes it and the process that breaks it. The plates are this made literal: the iron–carbon diagram is a map of the forces whose paths are every heat treatment of steel.

6. Economy of means

Mies van der Rohe, director, 1930–1933

One typeface, one working colour, everything lowercase, a dark theme that is the same page with ink and paper swapped and nothing else. Ornament is noise charged to the reader.

7. The workshops are the order

the Bauhaus Werkstätten

A material is filed under what it is, in a workshop — metal, clay and glass, yarn and rope, the preliminary course — not by product or market. A single vocabulary governs it, and an undeclared workshop is a hard error, not a silent typo.

where the facts come from

Every one of the photographs carries a licence and a source — the photographer's own page, an agency, a museum, a maker, or Wikimedia Commons where nothing else would do — and nothing with a non-commercial or no-derivatives licence is used. Figures are typical published values for the common grade, not a datasheet. A checker refuses, before anything ships, a card without its parts, an undeclared workshop, a bad DOI, a non-reusable licence, or a library text that claims the public domain with nowhere to read it.

open and free

No tracking, no advertising, no wall. The page is five scripts and works straight off the filesystem, even with JavaScript switched off, degraded to the raw readable data. The text and the tools are MIT; the photographs keep their own licences. The whole thing — the data, the checks, the history — is in the open at github.com/llama-with-thumbs/materials.
